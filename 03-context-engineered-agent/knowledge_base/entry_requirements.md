# Entry requirements for UK residents

> Simplified, illustrative guidance for the demo. Rules change - always check official government sources (e.g. GOV.UK foreign travel advice) before travelling.

## Travelling to the United States (ESTA)
UK citizens travelling to the USA (e.g. New York, Seattle, San Diego or Los Angeles) for tourism or business for up to 90 days usually need an approved ESTA under the Visa Waiver Program. Apply online at least 72 hours before departure. Passports must be valid for the duration of the stay. Travelling via Dublin allows US Preclearance before departure.

## Travelling to the Schengen area (Netherlands, France, Iceland)
UK citizens can visit the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. The EU Entry/Exit System (EES) registers fingerprints and a facial photo on first entry, which can add time at border control. Passports must be issued within the last 10 years and be valid for at least 3 months after the planned departure date. Iceland is in Schengen but not in the EU.

## Travelling to Ireland
Ireland and the UK form the Common Travel Area. UK citizens do not need a passport to travel to Ireland, but airlines usually require photo ID; a passport is recommended.

## Children travelling
Every child, including infants, needs their own passport for international travel. Children travelling with only one parent may be asked for a consent letter from the other parent.
